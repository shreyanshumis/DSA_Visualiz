Learn the operators
Here, ^ means XOR, not power. All bitwise operations use signed 32-bit integers. Parentheses make grouping explicit. FOR bounds are inclusive; PRINT outputs a decimal integer.
OperatorMeaningExample
&AND: both bits are 15 & 3 = 1
|OR: either bit is 15 | 3 = 7
^XOR: bits differ5 ^ 3 = 6
~Complement: invert all bits~5 = -6
<<Shift bits left5 << 1 = 10
>>Signed shift right5 >> 1 = 2
Why is 5 ^ 3 equal to 6?
5 = 0101
3 = 0011
^ = 0110 = 6
XOR returns 1 where the two input bits differ. Leading zeros do not change a positive value. Negative values are shown using 32-bit two’s complement.
